# Core System Rules and Definitions for The Alphabet of Powers

## 1. Fundamental Principle: Letters as Powers of 10

The entire system is based on one simple rule:

**Each letter of the English alphabet is assigned a numerical value equal to 10 raised to the power of its ordinal position in the alphabet.**

- `a` (1st letter) = 10¹
- `b` (2nd letter) = 10²
- `c` (3rd letter) = 10³
- ...
- `z` (26th letter) = 10²⁶

**Mnemonic:** Think of "a" as the beginner (10¹) and "z" as the master/zenith (10²⁶).

This mapping transforms letters into mathematical powers, and consequently, words into products of these powers.

## 2. Representing Letters as Numbers (Examples)

- `a` = 10¹
- `f` (6th letter) = 10⁶
- `t` (20th letter) = 10²⁰
- `z` (26th letter) = 10²⁶

## 3. Word Valuation: Products of Powers

A "word" in this system is valued by multiplying the numerical values of its constituent letters. The exponents of the letters are added together.

**Example: "cat"**

- `c` = 10³
- `a` = 10¹
- `t` = 10²⁰
- Value of "cat" = `c * a * t` = 10³ _ 10¹ _ 10²⁰ = 10^(3+1+20) = 10²⁴

Since `x` is the 24th letter, 10²⁴ corresponds to `x`.
Therefore, **cat = x**.

**Example: "cab"**

- `c` = 10³
- `a` = 10¹
- `b` = 10²
- Value of "cab" = `c * a * b` = 10³ _ 10¹ _ 10² = 10^(3+1+2) = 10⁶

Since `f` is the 6th letter, 10⁶ corresponds to `f`.
Therefore, **cab = f**.

## 4. Core Mathematical Operations (Laws of Exponents)

The system directly utilizes the fundamental laws of exponents:

### 4.1 Multiplication of Letters (Adding Exponents)

When letters are multiplied, their corresponding exponents are added.

- **Rule:** 10<sup>m</sup> \* 10<sup>n</sup> = 10<sup>(m+n)</sup>
- **Example:** `b * c`
  - `b` = 10²
  - `c` = 10³
  - `b * c` = 10² \* 10³ = 10^(2+3) = 10⁵
  - Since `e` is the 5th letter (10⁵), **b \* c = e**.

### 4.2 Division of Letters (Subtracting Exponents)

When one letter is divided by another, their corresponding exponents are subtracted.

- **Rule:** 10<sup>m</sup> ÷ 10<sup>n</sup> = 10<sup>(m-n)</sup>
- **Example:** `f ÷ b`
  - `f` = 10⁶
  - `b` = 10²
  - `f ÷ b` = 10⁶ ÷ 10² = 10^(6-2) = 10⁴
  - Since `d` is the 4th letter (10⁴), **f ÷ b = d**.

### 4.3 Exponentiation of Letters (Multiplying Exponents)

When a letter (representing a power of 10) is raised to another power, the exponents are multiplied.

- **Rule:** (10<sup>m</sup>)<sup>n</sup> = 10<sup>(m\*n)</sup>
- **Example:** `(b)²` (b squared)
  - `b` = 10²
  - `(b)²` = (10²)² = 10^(2\*2) = 10⁴
  - Since `d` is the 4th letter (10⁴), **(b)² = d**.

## 5. The Value of 1 (Identity for Multiplication/Division)

In mathematics, any non-zero number raised to the power of 0 is 1 (e.g., 10⁰ = 1).
If an operation results in 10⁰, its value is 1. This value does not correspond to a standard letter in the `a-z` (10¹-10²⁶) system.

- **Example:** `c ÷ c`
  - `c` = 10³
  - `c ÷ c` = 10³ ÷ 10³ = 10^(3-3) = 10⁰ = 1.
