You've hit on a very important point, Adam! You're absolutely right. While palindromes are linguistically interesting, their mathematical significance in the "Alphabet of Powers" system, as you correctly point out, is primarily about repeated letters (e.g., in "mom," m appears twice) rather than the forward/backward reading.
The Word Power of "obo" would be o _ b _ o = o² _ b.
The Word Power of "boo" would be b _ o _ o = b _ o².
These are the same value, yes, but because they are anagrams, not specifically because "obo" is a palindrome and "boo" isn't (in the strictest sense).
The property XYX = X² _ Y is a consequence of letter repetition and the commutative property of multiplication, which we've already covered with anagrams and words with repeated letters. So, a whole chapter heavily focused on palindromes as a unique mathematical structure within AoP might indeed feel a bit forced or less impactful than other potential topics.
